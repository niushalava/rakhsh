THE SHAHNAMEH

The Book of Kings

The Shahnameh is the great epic of Persia, written by the poet Ferdowsi around a thousand years ago. In more than 50,000 couplets it tells the history of Iran from the first kings to the coming of Islam — and at its heart stands Rustam, the greatest hero of them all.

THE SEVEN LABOURS

When King Kay Kavus and his army were captured and blinded by the demons of Mazandaran, Rustam rode alone down the short, deadly road to rescue them — a road of seven trials, the Haft Khan:

  1. The Lion — Rakhsh, Rustam's faithful horse, kills a lion while Rustam sleeps.
  2. The Desert — Dying of thirst, Rustam follows a ram to a hidden spring.
  3. The Dragon — A dragon comes three times in the night; at last Rustam sees it and cuts off its head.
  4. The Witch — A witch comes disguised as a beauty; Rustam names God, her true face appears, and he slays her.
  5. Olad — Rustam captures the warrior Olad, who becomes his guide to the White Div.
  6. Arzhang — Rustam storms the demons' camp and tears off the head of Arzhang, their chief.
  7. The White Div — Rustam wakes the White Div in its cave, wrestles it, and slays it — and its blood restores the sight of the blinded king and his army.

In Lumink, Ember walks the same road — seven khans, drawn in ink.

The verses in the game are from the Shahnameh in the English translation of Arthur and Edmond Warner (1906).
